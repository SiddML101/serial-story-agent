The steel jaws of the shears bite through the yellow polyethylene tape with a dry, plastic snap. The severed ends lash back against the doorframe, curling like dying worms.

"That's it, then," Marlo says, his voice dropping an octave as he grips the handlebars of his own bike. "You're in the dark now, Leo. The precinct doesn't ignore torn seals."

"The precinct didn't write this manifest," Leo says. He wedges his boot against the bottom of the door and pushes.

The wood gives way with a wet, splintering groan. The doorframe's fractured molding exposes a nest of dead woodlice, dry as husks. No lights are on inside. The dark of Apartment 4B smells of damp drywall and cold iron.

Leo grabs his handlebars, dragging his heavy cargo bicycle over the threshold. The front tire leaves a slick, black track on the dusty floorboards. He keeps the frame close, his knuckles white on the grips; the forty-eight-hundred-volt proximity lock is a silent threat hummed through the metal. He cannot leave the bike behind on the landing.

Marlo follows, his boots crunching on grit. "I don't hear anyone breathing, kid. If Kallen was moved here, he isn't making noise."

Leo reaches into his canvas courier bag, his fingers finding the wax-sealed manila envelope. The paper is stiff, cold to the touch. "Keep watch on the stairs."

"I'm watching," Marlo mutters, his eyes darting back to the dim hallway. "But this place... it smells like a cellar that's been boarded up since the factory fires."

Leo steers the bicycle deeper into the flat. The hallway opens into a narrow living room stripped of furniture. In the center of the bare floorboards stands a single object: a tubular chrome gurney, its hydraulic column stained with dark grease. The leather safety straps hang loose, their brass buckles pooling on the floorboards like spilled coins.

The gurney is empty.

On his handlebars, the terminal's screen flashes. The green glow turns a deep, bruised emerald, casting long shadows against the peeling walls. A sharp, rhythmic chime rings out—three high-pitched beeps that signal a successful location match.

*DELIVERY 2/5: LOCATION CONFIRMED.*  
*RECIPIENT: KALLEN, D.*  
*STATUS: DECEASED. LOGGED 03:42 A.M.*  

"It's registering him," Leo whispers, his thumb hovering over the keypad. "The terminal thinks he's right here."

"He isn't," Marlo says, stepping back toward the door. "Look at the straps, Leo. They didn't undo themselves."

Before Leo can drop the wax-sealed envelope onto the empty gurney, the heavy fire door behind them slams shut with a concussive bang that rattles the windowpanes. 

The rusted casters of the gurney shriek against the floorboards. The metal frame shudders, its front wheels spinning as the empty gurney begins to roll slowly, deliberately, toward Leo's front tire.
