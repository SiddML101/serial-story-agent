# Episode 6

> **Planned beat:** Marlo Briggs intercepts Leo at The Broken Chain Diner, forcing a tense conversation about why midnight shifts attract vanishing couriers; Marlo refuses to let Leo walk alone.

**Hook (threat):** An automated terminal discharge fires a blinding arc of blue electricity directly into Marlo's heavy iron tow chain as Leo flees the diner.  
**In-story time:** Day 2, 10:15 pm

---

Leo wheels his heavy bicycle straight into the narrow entryway of The Broken Chain Diner, keeping the handlebars wedged hard against his ribs. The terminal on his steering stem hums a steady warning, tracking the distance to his frame lock. 

"You're dragging the leash, Vance," a gravelly voice rasps from the corner booth.

Marlo Briggs slumps behind a Formica table littered with cold fry grease. A deep indigo tally mark, tattooed directly over his left jawbone, twitches as he chews a cold cigarette stub. He gestures with his chin toward the front wheel jammed in the doorframe. "Step six inches further into the aisle and Gideon’s board cooks you from the inside out."

Leo stops, his boots sliding on the wet linoleum. He reaches into his jacket lining, pulls out the continuous-form yellowed routing manifest, and slaps it onto the table between them.

"Parcel two was a freight locker full of whispering voices, Marlo," Leo says, keeping his voice low and tight. "This manifest was in my bag. Old routes, stamped with the snake-and-keyhole. It pulled a mortuary file for my first stop."

Marlo doesn't touch the paper. He stares at the dark wax seal printed at the top. "Put it back in your coat."

"Harlan Reed was pronounced dead at two-fifteen," Leo says, leaning over his top tube. "He took his delivery an hour later. What is Shift Zero?"

Marlo unzips his oilskin coat, revealing a heavy steel tow chain wrapped three times around his waist. "It's the graveyard shift beneath the real one. Standard couriers deliver to the living. Shift Zero runs on the closed analog network under the city mortuary."

"Gideon locked my terminal into it," Leo says. "The board shocked me when I tried to pull an emergency override."

"Gideon doesn't pick who gets routed," Marlo says, leaning across the table, his breath sour with stale chicory. "The algorithm selects a rider when the backlog builds up. Seven couriers have been assigned Shift Zero since I started riding fifteen years ago. You know how many finished all five drops?"

"None," Leo says.

"Three abandoned their rigs—found their bikes melted to slag in the gutter," Marlo says. "Two went off the river bridge. The other two just stopped answering dispatch halfway through."

"I'm not dying for a FlashDrop contract."

"You don't get to decline a shift once the terminal uploads it," Marlo says. He slides out of the booth, unhooking the heavy tow chain from his belt with a sharp metallic clatter. "But I'm not letting another rider walk into that network alone. Roll out."

"You're coming with me?"

"I'm staying on your rear tire until we figure out where that manifest came from," Marlo says, looping the chain around his left forearm.

Before Leo can turn his handlebars back toward the street, his waterproof earpiece bursts into high-pitched screeching. Gideon's voice cuts through the feedback, smooth and utterly cold.

"Rider Vance. Unscheduled stop detected. You have ninety seconds to resume your route before automated terminal discharge initiates."

Marlo grabs Leo's shoulder, shoving him toward the downpour. "Out. Now."

Leo slams through the glass door into the rain just as a sharp metallic snap sparks from his front axle. The bicycle's front tire locks dead, and a blinding arc of blue electricity shoots from the terminal, jumping directly into Marlo's heavy iron tow chain.
