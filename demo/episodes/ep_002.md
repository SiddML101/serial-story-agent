The steel frame of the bicycle vibrates between Leo’s thighs, a cold, continuous tremor that matches the rhythmic pulsing of the terminal. He swerves onto Third Avenue, his thin tires cutting through slick grease and pooling sleet. The wind off the canal smells of sulfur and wet coal.

Every rotation of the pedals feels heavy, as though the chain is dragging through wet cement. On his handlebars, the ruggedized display remains locked on the violet screen. *SHIFT ZERO. 1/5.* The destination flashes in a jagged digital font: *142 Crane Street, Apt 2A.*

Two blocks from the address, Leo slides the bike into a narrow alley beneath the skeletal platform of an old fire escape. Water cascades off the iron grating above, drumming a frantic rhythm against his nylon helmet. He stands straddling the top tube, his breath blooming in white, ragged plumes.

The high-voltage hum in the handlebars has subsided, leaving only a faint, greasy warmth in the rubber grips. Leo unclips the heavy plastic buckles of his roll-top cargo bag. The waterproof vinyl peels back to reveal the dark interior.

Resting at the bottom is the heavy, wax-sealed manila envelope Gideon slid through the mesh. The dark red wax is stamped with a nameless, circular logo—a snake swallowing its own tail around a stylized keyhole. 

But as Leo reaches for the envelope, his fingers brush against something loose in the corner of the bag. Something cold.

He pulls his hand back, then reaches deeper, his raw knuckles scraping the stiff vinyl liner. He drags the object into the dim light of the alley.

It is an iron latch key.

The thing is heavy, old-fashioned, and choked with a thick, velvety coat of bright orange rust. As Leo holds it in his bare palm, the metal begins to buzz. The vibration is microscopic but intense, a high-frequency tickle that sends a needle of static heat straight into the fresh burn on his thumb.

The rust isn't dry. It rubs off on his skin in a wet, dark brown paste that smells of stagnant river water and copper. He wipes his palm against his trousers, but the metallic stink remains, clinging to his skin like grease.

He looks from the vibrating key in his hand to the wax-sealed envelope addressed to Apartment 2A. The delivery instructions on his terminal offer no explanation, only the ticking countdown.

Who left this key in his bag, and what lies behind the door it is meant to unlock?
