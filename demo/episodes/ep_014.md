"Move your bike, Vance," Marlo snaps, grabbing the rubber grip of Leo’s handlebars. "Gideon isn't throwing empty threats. If he shifts the terminal cap, the shock will fry your frame."

"He's stalling," Leo says, wrenching the handlebars straight to lock the front wheel against the doorframe. "He doesn't want us touching what's behind this wire."

Leo reaches out and unclips the heavy iron keyring hanging from Marlo's belt loop. Marlo curses under his breath, but he doesn't pull away, his head swiveling toward the dark corridor behind them.

Beside a pile of rusted brass key blanks on a lower shelf lies a discarded rubber thumb slip, yellowed and worn paper-thin at the tip. Leo ignores it, stepping past the jammed front wheel into the narrow, unlit aisle of Cage Seven.

The air smells of soot and cold mortar. Rows of high pine racks stretch six deep into the darkness, bowed under thick stacks of uncollected freight. Leo runs his fingers across coarse burlap and damp, rotting cardboard until his hand hits a heavy wooden crate stamped with red stenciled ink: *SHIFTS 01-05 RETURNED / UNCLAIMED*.

"We have twenty seconds before the security beam sweeps back," Marlo whispers from the doorway, his boots scraping against the concrete floor. "Find the paper manifest and get out."

Leo reaches into the crate and pulls out three heavy packages. Each one is wrapped in thick brown paper, bound tight with hemp twine, and sealed with brittle gray wax stamps. When his thumb touches the wax, it crumbles into chalky dust that clings to his skin.

He tilts the top parcel toward the dim corridor light. The shipping label is printed in sharp black ink:
*RECIPIENT: V. KRANE — ASHWOOD MONOLITH, APT 812 [DECEASED]*

He shifts the second package into view:
*RECIPIENT: M. SALLOW — ASHWOOD MONOLITH, APT 14F [DECEASED]*

And the third:
*RECIPIENT: E. VANE — ASHWOOD MONOLITH, APT 301 [DECEASED]*

"They're all Ashwood addresses," Leo says, feeling the chalky wax smudge beneath his fingers. "Every single returned parcel."

"Leo," Marlo says. His voice drops low, stripped of its usual rough edge. "Put them back."

"Not until we check the manifests."

"Look down the aisle," Marlo whispers, stepping into the cage and grabbing Leo's shoulder with a rigid grip.

Past the last row of racks, where the timber frame meets the raw brickwork of the rear foundation, a dark alcove sits swallowed in shadow. A slow, dragging rasp cuts through the cellar noise—the heavy slide of waterlogged cloth pulling over wet gravel.

A freezing draft sweeps out of the gap, carrying the reek of copper and damp earth. From the black void of the alcove, a dark shape slides forward into the threshold of the aisle.
