# Episode 9

> **Planned beat:** Leo and Marlo climb the rotting stairs to Apartment 4B together, finding police tape stretched across the doorway while whispering about who tipped off the authorities.

Marlo pushes through the heavy fire door on the third-floor landing, his oilskin coat dripping grease-colored water onto the concrete. 

"You shouldn't have followed me from the diner," Leo says, keeping his shoulder pressed against the frame of his heavy cargo bicycle.

"The police band went crazy the second your terminal glitched," Marlo grunts, his chest heaving as he grips the iron balustrade. "Gideon's entire dispatch board just went dark. When the mortuary database overrides the hub like this, it doesn't just reroute you. It clears the board. I've seen riders try to walk away during a loop. The terminal discharges before they can even reach the street."

"Mira is downstairs in Kallen's old flat," Leo says, heaving the front wheel up the concrete steps. "She found the transfer slip. Gideon had Kallen moved into Apartment 4B while he was still breathing. But dispatch just told me they have no record of that address."

"Gideon doesn't know half of what the network does," Marlo says, his boots heavy on the risers. "But he didn't sign that transfer slip. FlashDrop doesn't have the authority to move a dying courier. That comes from the top."

"Arthur Vane," Leo says.

"He owns the concrete under your soles, kid. And he doesn't leave loose ends."

They reach the fourth-floor landing. The light is a dying amber flicker from a single bare bulb overhead. The floor here is covered in a grid of water-logged green linoleum tiles that exhale a sharp, cabbage-like rot wherever Leo's front tire rolls.

Directly ahead, the door to Apartment 4B stands in the shadows. Stretched across the dark wood are two diagonal bands of bright yellow polyethylene tape, stapled hard into the molding. Stamped in black block letters is the warning: *POLICE LINE - DO NOT CROSS.*

Leo lowers the bike, the kickstand scraping the floorboards. "The police? How did they get here so fast?"

"Look at the staples," Marlo whispers, leaning close to the doorframe. "They aren't even rusted yet. This tape went up within the last hour. Someone wanted this unit sealed tight before your terminal redirected you."

"Gideon?"

"No. Gideon needs you to complete the drop to clear his own console," Marlo says, his eyes darting toward the dark stairwell leading up to the fifth floor. "If you don't log the delivery, the terminal stays locked on his end too. Someone else tipped off the precinct to block the route. Someone who wants Kallen to stay lost."

The handlebar terminal suddenly emits a low, rhythmic chime. On the green screen, the text blinks: *PROXIMITY LOCKED. DEPOSIT PARCEL.*

Leo reaches into his pocket and pulls out his heavy-duty steel wire-cutters. He aligns the blades with the taut yellow plastic tape. 

"Don't do it, Leo," Marlo warns, his hand clamping down on Leo's wrist. "You cut that seal, and you're not just breaking a company rule. You're crossing a crime scene."

Leo clamps the cold metal jaws of the shears over the yellow tape and squeezes.
