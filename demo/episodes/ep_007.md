# Episode 7

> **Planned beat:** At Apartment 3F, Leo finds the door wide open where Mira Chen is already photographing an uncollected newspaper; they argue in urgent whispers over the dead tenant's FlashDrop jacket.

"Kill the flash, Mira," Leo whispers, jamming his front wheel over the threshold of Apartment 3F.

"I need the exposure," Mira whispers back, her voice tight as she adjusts the mechanical twin-lens camera pressed to her chin. A sharp *click-clack* of the shutter cuts through the damp apartment. "And keep your boots off the entry rug."

Leo shifts his weight. The wet canvas strap of his heavy courier bag has chafed his collarbone into a raw, salt-rimed pink welt that burns with every breath. He stays glued to the bicycle, the handlebar terminal chirping its low, rhythmic proximity ping against his ribs.

"Gideon is running live voltage checks on my tracker," Leo says, his eyes scanning the dark hallway. "If dispatch pings you in an active drop zone—"

"Dispatch isn't watching me. They’re erasing this flat." Mira aims her lens down at an uncollected copy of the *Oakhaven Evening Post* rotting near the radiator, its front page swollen into a grey pulp. "Look at the headline folds. Nobody picked up the morning edition after October fourth."

"I'm on a timed run," Leo says, his voice a harsh, low scrape. "What are you doing here?"

"Following the paper trail." Mira rises, her dark eyes flashing as she points her camera toward the kitchen table. "And they left evidence behind."

Draped over the back of a splintered chair is an insulated nylon jacket—FlashDrop safety orange, grease-stained at the elbows, with reflective silver bands peeling along the zipper flap.

Leo leans as far as his frame leash permits, his hand hovering over the faded nylon. "That's standard courier issue. A winter shell."

"Don't touch it," Mira snaps, stepping between him and the chair. "You think you're delivering to random dead tenants? The man who lived here died in that chair twenty days ago. Emphysema and acute heart failure."

"Then why does he have our fleet gear?" Leo demands, his fingers curling. "FlashDrop doesn't issue these shells to civilians. Gideon collects them when a rider quits."

"He didn't quit," she says, leaning close enough that the sharp, vinegary smell of darkroom fixer on her wool sweater cuts through the damp room. "I pulled his county burial permit from the city archives this afternoon. Look at the brass name-tag clipped over the storm flap. Read it, Leo."

Leo uses the tip of his pen to flip the heavy collar forward. Stamped into the tarnished metal plate is an employee number and a name: *D. KALLEN – ROUTE RELIEF*.

"Kallen," Leo says, a knot tightening in his gut. "He covered the northern district before I started. He vanished three weeks ago."

"He didn't vanish," Mira whispers, her jaw set. "The coroner's report was forged on FlashDrop letterhead, and the death certificate listed Apartment 3F to close out his contract."

"Then whose body did they bury?"

Mira pulls a damp carbon copy of a police transfer slip from her coat pocket. She turns it toward the window, pointing past the fire escape toward the brick facade directly opposite them.

"Nobody," Mira says. "The mortuary van never picked him up from 3F. The transfer slip shows dispatch ordered him moved across the lightwell while he was still breathing—straight into Apartment 4B."
