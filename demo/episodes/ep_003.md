# Episode 3

> **Planned beat:** At Apartment 2A, the tenant receives the key in eerie silence, leaving a cold damp film on Leo's palm during the handover.

**Hook (revelation):** Leo discovers that his first delivery recipient, Harlan Reed, was pronounced dead at 02:15 a.m., an hour before the delivery was made.

Leo shoves the vibrating key into his jacket pocket and grabs the wax-sealed envelope. 142 Crane Street is a four-story walk-up built from water-logged brick that bleeds salt efflorescence into the mortar.

He pushes through the heavy front door. The lobby carpet is stiff with black grease and road grit. Up on the second floor, the hallway floorboards groan beneath his sodden work boots. The smell here is static electricity, damp plaster, and old lard.

Apartment 2A sits at the far end of the corridor. The brass numbers mounted on the wood are pitted with greenish corrosion.

Leo knocks three times. Hard, heavy raps that vibrate back into his knuckles.

No footsteps approach. No lock clicks.

Instead, the brass knob turns on its own, smoothly and soundlessly, as if greased with soft fat. The heavy oak door glides inward three inches, stopping against a brass security chain. The narrow gap reveals only deep shadow.

"FlashDrop," Leo says, his voice flat and raspy from the rain. "Delivery for two-A."

Silence fills the space between them. No sound of breathing, no shifting weight, only a thin draft that carries the scent of formaldehyde and scorched dust.

The gap widens slightly. A pale, hairless hand extends slowly from the darkness through the chained opening. The fingers are long, thin, and rigid, the yellowed nails caked with black sediment.

Leo pulls the rusted key from his pocket. The tiny vibration in the iron pulses against his skin, frantic now. He lays the heavy iron key onto the extended palm alongside the wax-sealed manila envelope.

The thin fingers wrap around both items instantly.

During the transfer, the tenant’s cold flesh brushes the soft flat of Leo's palm. The touch is frigid—like pressing skin against a frozen pipe in an unheated basement. When the hand retracts into the dark, it leaves a slick, ice-cold film covering Leo’s skin, translucent and greasy, smelling of stagnant pond mud.

The door pulls shut with a sharp, heavy clack of the brass latch.

Leo rubs his palm against his work trousers, but the damp smear does not come off; it clings to his skin like silicone, freezing his hand to the bone.

He turns back toward the stairwell, lifting his handlebar terminal to clear the delivery. The violet screen flashes twice, clearing the first entry with a soft, mechanical chirp.

*SHIFT ZERO. 2/5.*

A fresh line of system text scrolls across the ruggedized screen, pulling data straight from the mortuary's offline legacy server:

*142 CRANE ST, APT 2A: COMPLETED.*
*RECIPIENT: HARLAN REED.*
*MORTALITY RECORD: PRONOUNCED DEAD AT 02:15 A.M. CAUSE: CARDIAC ARREST.*

Leo checks his watch. It is 3:22 a.m. The man who took the key died barely an hour before Leo stepped onto his porch.
