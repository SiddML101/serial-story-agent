# Episode 4

> **Planned beat:** Leo attempts to cancel his next assignment, but his bicycle handlebars deliver an electric shock as Gideon warns him over the headset that shifts cannot be altered.

**Words:** 496 · **Version:** v2 · **Human edited:** no · **Revisions:** 0 · **Critic:** hook 5/5, momentum 4/5

**Hook (threat):** Gideon reveals that walking more than thirty feet from the bike before finishing the deliveries will trigger a lethal forty-eight-hundred-volt discharge into Leo's body.  
**In-story time:** Day 1, 02:30 a.m.

---

Leo stumbles down the steps of 142 Crane Street, his right palm completely numb. The icy grease Harlan Reed left on his skin refuses to rub off against his heavy canvas jacket, sealing his skin like cold varnish.

Rain hits the back of his neck as he steps into the alley where his bicycle rests. He kicks the stand back and stares at the handlebar terminal. The violet display blinks, clearing the first stop and pulling data straight from the mortuary network.

*SHIFT ZERO. 2/5.*
*DESTINATION: LOCKER 412 – 5TH STREET FREIGHT TERMINAL.*

"No," Leo mutters into the downpour. 

He grips the heavy rubber casing of the ruggedized screen. His thumbs press down hard on the dual emergency-abort buttons mounted beneath the display housing—the physical override designed to pull a courier off a dangerous run. He holds them down, waiting for the system diagnostic prompt.

The terminal screeches, a high-pitched metallic whine. A line of corrupted text overrides the destination: *ROUTING REFUSAL UNSUPPORTED.*

Leo jams both thumbs harder into the toggle buttons. "Abort shift. Clear route."

A bright, blue-white arc snaps from the iron frame directly into his bare knuckles. The shock travels straight up his forearms like a strike from a steel bar, violently throwing his hands clear of the grips. His chest muscles spasm, locking his breath in his throat; his jaw snaps shut so hard his molars click. He hits the wet pavement on one knee, gasping as the smell of burnt hair and ozone fills his nose.

Before he can push himself up, his waterproof earpiece crackles. Static sweeps through the speaker, followed by the dry, measured breathing of Gideon Vance-Cross.

"I warned you about the static in the frame, Vance," Gideon says. The dispatcher’s voice is muffled by the distant, heavy hum of the hub's cooling fans. "The Shift Zero algorithm doesn't accept manual inputs from the field. You know the protocols."

Leo pushes himself off the slick asphalt, his left hand shaking as he clutches his scorched right wrist. "The tenant at Crane Street was dead, Gideon. Pronounced an hour ago. The manifest pulled a mortuary file."

"The dispatch system processes completed manifests," Gideon replies, his tone cold and immaculate. "What the network routes, you deliver. You don't audit the chain of custody."

"I'm dropping the bag," Leo says, his breath ragged. "Find another rider."

"Look at the screen, Vance."

Leo glances up at the handlebars. The violet backlighting has turned a deep, warning red. A digital voltage bar fills steadily toward the margin, buzzing at a frequency that vibrates the front tire against the wet gravel.

"The circuit is grounded through your terminal lock," Gideon’s voice drops into a flat, deliberate register. "If you step more than thirty feet from that frame before delivery five is logged, the terminal discharges the full forty-eight hundred volts into the frame wire. It won't just burn your hands this time, Leo. It will stop your heart before your boots hit the curb."

The terminal rejects the command and delivers a violent electrical shock to his hands. Gideon Vance-Cross contacts Leo through his earpiece, stating that the Shift Zero algorithm refuses manual inputs and that Leo must complete the remaining deliveries. When Leo threatens to drop the delivery bag, Gideon reveals that the terminal is hardwired to a lethal forty-eight-hundred-volt charge that will trigger if Leo steps more than thirty feet away from the bike before delivery five.
