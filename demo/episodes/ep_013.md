The heavy mesh door of Cage Seven rests open against the concrete sill, its bent hinges groaning in the basement draft.

Marlo stumbles back, holding the snapped stub of the iron skeleton key. "The trip-wire," he growls, eyes locked on the open vault. "The cylinder didn't jam by accident. Gideon set an anti-pick charge."

"He knows we're here," Leo says, dragging his cargo bike past the doorway to block the entrance from the corridor side.

Inside the cage, row after row of wooden crates sit under thick dust, marked with faded red stencil numbers. Beside a rusting terminal unit from the eighties, a crust of chalky white lead-salts flakes off a cracked glass battery jar, dripping sulfurous sludge onto the brickwork. The sharp, bitter tang of battery acid hits Leo's throat, making his eyes water.

"Don't touch the shelves," Marlo warns, grabbing Leo’s sleeve to hold him back. "He’s got the whole mortuary relay tied into this floor. If you step past the threshold without clearing the terminal, the analog board trips."

"We're already past the threshold, Marlo," Leo whispers, scanning the dark spaces between the high shelving racks.

Before Marlo can reply, a dry static hiss erupts from a wall-mounted horn speaker—an abandoned PA unit whose copper wiring hangs cut three inches below the metal housing.

A smooth, calm voice cuts through the pop of dead air.

"You always were predictable, Vance," Gideon’s voice comes through the horn speaker, crisp and clipped despite the severed wire. "And you, Marlo. I gave you fifteen years of quiet shifts at the diner. I thought you had enough sense to stay out of the sub-basement."

Marlo steps toward the speaker, his fists tight at his sides. "You locked Kallen in 4B, Gideon. You forged his burial permit while he was still breathing."

"Courier Kallen failed to complete his cycle," Gideon says, his voice devoid of emotion, like an administrative clerk reading an invoice. "The routing algorithm does not accommodate incomplete drops. When a courier refuses to finish Shift Zero, the network clears the ledger by other means."

Leo steps up beside Marlo, his knuckles white against his handlebars. "The terminal didn't redirect to Locker 412 by accident. It dragged us straight to 4B because someone pushed a manual loop from outside."

A short, dry laugh crackles from the speaker. "You think you're investigating a crime, Leo? You're merely performing routine maintenance. Step away from Cage Seven now. Pull your bike back to the main ramp, surrender the manifest, and log off."

"And if we don't?" Leo asks.

"Then your third delivery won't be in the city," Gideon replies instantly. "The algorithm will override your terminal voltage cap. Delivery 3/5 will be routed to the south industrial docks—the unpaved piers where the current takes six days to carry anything to the bay. You have ten seconds to step out of the vault."

The line drops into silence with a soft metallic click.

Marlo looks at Leo, his face pale in the gloom. "He’s not bluffing. The dock routes are dead zones. No signal, no witnesses. We fall back."

Leo looks from Marlo to the dark, narrow aisles of Cage Seven. Instead of retreating toward the ramp, Leo grips his handlebars, shoves the heavy front tire of his cargo bike past the threshold, and wedges the steel frame firmly into the doorway, jamming the gate wide open and locking them inside.
