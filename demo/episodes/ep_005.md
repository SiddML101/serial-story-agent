Leo keeps one hand squeezed around the rubber handlebar grip, pulling the heavy bicycle frame along the rain-slick curb. The red backlighting of the terminal display casts a bloody, flickering glow across the wet asphalt. Every five seconds, the ruggedized housing emits a sharp chirp—a proximity ping tracking the distance between his chest and the frame lock.

His burnt knuckles throb. On his right palm, the freezing grease from Harlan Reed’s doorway has hardened into a thin, glassy veneer that refuses to scrape off against his canvas coat.

He turns onto 5th Street. The freight terminal looms out of the downpour like a stranded barge: forty acres of corrugated steel, dead loading bays, and sodium lamps buzzing in the fog. Water streams off the rusted skyways overhead, hitting the pavement in heavy, greasy sheets.

*SHIFT ZERO. 2/5.*
*DESTINATION: LOCKER 412 – 5TH STREET FREIGHT TERMINAL.*

Leo straddles the frame, pedaling slowly up the concrete ramp toward the exterior delivery alcove. The algorithm’s red voltage bar holds steady on the handlebar screen, humming at a pitch that makes the fillings in his molars ache.

The automated drop-box wall sits under a low overhang. Three hundred steel lockers, painted olive drab, stretch down the damp tiled passage. Most of the doors are dented, sealed with rusted padlocks, or coated in heavy axle grease to keep out the salt air.

He jams his front tire hard against the base of the locker matrix, keeping the frame well within the thirty-foot lethal boundary.

Locker 412 sits chest-high in the middle column. Its steel face is stained with brown grease that smells of burnt diesel and old tallow. Mounted directly above the lock mechanism is a square brass intercom grill, its perforated mesh choked with soot.

Leo unzips his courier bag. Tucked beside the remaining parcels is an extra slip of paper he hadn't noticed before—a continuous-form routing manifest printed on yellowed paper. It carries the snake-and-keyhole logo, listing historical delivery addresses that date back decades, each crossed out in red wax.

He reaches for the locker handle, but a sound stops his hand an inch from the cold steel.

It emanates from behind the grease-stained brass intercom grill.

Not system static. Not the hum of the city power grid.

It is the sound of muffled human whispering. Dozens of distinct voices, hushed and frantic, speaking all at once in rapid, overlapping murmurs vibrating through the iron housing.

Leo leans closer, bringing his ear near the soot-clogged mesh.

"...manifest four-eight... terminal temperature declining... clear the line..."

A thin draft vents through the holes, smelling of damp lime and scorched copper.

Leo grips the handle of Locker 412 and pulls. The heavy steel door swings wide on its greased hinges, revealing the dark, hollow interior where the delivery must go.
