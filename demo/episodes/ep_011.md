The gurney's rusted casters lock, skidding across the bare pine floorboards with a high-pitched wail. A sharp reek of scorched rubber rises as the heavy chrome frame lunges directly toward Leo’s front tire. 

Through the green glare of his terminal screen, Leo spots a jagged fracture across the gurney's black vinyl headrest, exposing yellowed foam that has rotted into a dry, honeycomb structure. The frame tracks his movement with unnatural precision.

"Leo, move!" Marlo yells, shoving a short steel tire iron into the gap between the fire door and the splintered jamb. 

Marlo throws his shoulder against the iron bar, his boots slipping on the grimy green linoleum. 

The gurney slams into the front rack of Leo’s cargo bicycle. The impact sends a violent shock up the handlebars, shooting cold static into Leo’s wrists. The proximity terminal on his grips screams a single continuous tone.

"It's pinning the frame!" Leo shoves his hip against the top tube, fighting the heavy metal. The gurney's loose leather straps lash against his tire, wild and kinetic.

With a dry, metallic crack, Marlo shears the door latch. He grabs Leo by the collar of his canvas jacket and yanks him backward over the threshold. 

They tumble onto the concrete landing of the stairwell, dragging the heavy cargo bike behind them. The fire door swings shut with a pneumatic hiss, locking with a heavy thud that silences the shrieking wheels inside.

Leo lies on the cold concrete, his chest heaving as he breathes in the smell of burnt rubber clinging to his sleeves. Above them, a bare bulb flickers, throwing jagged shadows against the flaking yellow paint of the stairwell.

"That wasn't Kallen," Leo says, pushing himself up against the iron balustrade. "The terminal logged his death at 03:42 A.M., but that room was empty."

"It’s the algorithm," Marlo says, wiping sweat from his forehead with his sleeve. "The mortuary database is forcing a closure. It doesn't need a body to complete a drop; it just needs a target."

Leo reaches into his canvas bag, his fingers finding the stiff, cold edges of the wax-sealed envelope Gideon prepared. "And this? What happens to the parcels we can't deliver?"

"They go back to the source," Marlo says, his voice dropping as he steps closer. "The depot basement. Cage Seven. That’s where the undeliverable manifests and returned packages are locked before they're logged as dead inventory. If you want to know what Gideon is really making us carry, we have to raid that cage."

"Gideon keeps the basement gate key on his belt," Leo says. "And he's watching the hub console."

Marlo reaches deep into his heavy coat and pulls out a thick steel keyring. Hanging from the loop is a tarnished brass oval stamped with four crude punch-letters: *Courier 4409*.

Leo stares at the brass tag. "That's Kallen's ID badge."

"Kallen gave it to me the night before he vanished into Apartment 3F," Marlo says, his eyes dark in the amber light. "He said if he didn't complete Shift Zero, the proof was already sitting inside Cage Seven."
