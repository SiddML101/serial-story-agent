# Episode 1

> **Planned beat:** Leo Vance accepts a late-night delivery run at the FlashDrop hub, only to discover his terminal lock-screen forces a route onto his device without his consent.

**Words:** 479 · **Version:** v2 · **Human edited:** no · **Revisions:** 0 · **Critic:** hook 5/5, momentum 5/5

**Hook (cliffhanger):** Gideon watches coldly as Leo's terminal counts down to a potential high-voltage discharge while locked onto a mandatory shift.  
**In-story time:** Day 1, 3:00 am

---

Leo pulls the nylon strap tight, the plastic buckle clicking like a dry bone. Water from his rain jacket drips onto the concrete floor of the FlashDrop basement depot, mixing with spilled hydraulic fluid to form iridescent oil slicks. The smell of ozone and wet wool clings to the low ceiling.

Behind the wire-mesh partition, Gideon Vance-Cross stands under a flickering halogen tube, his grey suit immaculate despite the three-a.m. shift. He is sliding a heavy, wax-sealed manila envelope into a plastic security sleeve.

"Your terminal is loaded, Vance," Gideon says. His voice is smooth, polished, and entirely out of place in a basement that smells of industrial grease and damp rot. "Standard protocol applies. Direct routes. No deviations."

Leo wipes grease from his temple with the back of his hand. His knuckles are raw from the autumn wind. "The rain's turning to sleet out there, Gideon. My shifter is slipping on the third sprocket. Give me the North District run. It's flat."

"The system assigns the runs," Gideon says. He doesn't look up from his ledger. He slides the security sleeve through the slot in the wire mesh. "The system knows what you owe."

The terminal mounted to Leo’s handlebars chirps. It is an old, ruggedized unit, its screen scarred by key-scratches and road grit. Leo taps the power button, expecting the usual menu of optional shifts. Instead, the screen flashes a harsh, violet light.

A single prompt fills the display: *SHIFT ZERO. 5 DELIVERIES.*

Below it, the "Accept" box is already checked. The "Decline" button is greyed out, buried beneath a string of system errors.

Leo taps the touch screen. Nothing. He presses the physical override toggle on the side of the casing. The plastic is burning hot. A sharp, metallic hum vibrates through the aluminum handlebars of his bicycle, making his teeth ache.

"This is glitched," Leo says, tapping the glass harder. "It's locking me out of the main menu. Reset the server from your side."

Gideon finally looks up. His eyes are cold, dark pools under the halogen glare. "I don't reset the server, Leo. Once a run is locked, it's yours. You know the rules."

"I didn't press accept," Leo says.

A sudden, sharp spark leaps from the terminal’s charging port, biting into Leo’s thumb. He jerks his hand back, the smell of singed skin rising between them. On the screen, a digital countdown begins, ticking down from sixty seconds in jagged, red numbers. 

The hum in the handlebars grows stronger, turning into a high-pitched whine that vibrates through his collarbones. The metal of the frame is warming up, transferring a prickling static heat through his grip. If he doesn't mount the bike and break the connection to the depot's local receiver, the terminal will dump its high-voltage charge straight through his hands. 

"Fifty seconds, Leo," Gideon says, his voice flat. "I'd start pedaling."
