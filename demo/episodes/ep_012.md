"Keep your head down," Marlo whispers, pulling Leo behind a concrete pillar.

Above them, a hydraulic condensation pipe thrums with a low vibration that rattles through the soles of Leo's boots. Dripping from a structural beam overhead, pale glycerine leaks slowly from the cracked glass face of an abandoned pressure gauge, pooling on the floor in viscous beads. Fifty feet ahead, the yellow sweep of an automated security sensor cuts through the sub-basement gloom, sweeping the damp brickwork in a sulfurous glare.

Leo crouches beside his cargo bike, holding the brake levers tight to keep the cables from clinking against the frame. The proximity terminal on his grips remains dark, but the raw metal casing radiates a dry, electric heat against his hands.

"The sweep passes every forty seconds," Marlo says, his eyes fixed on the moving beam. "We have twenty to clear the corridor."

"Cage Seven is past the boilers?" Leo asks.

"It's where the dead weight sits," Marlo grunts. "Parcels that couldn't hit their targets because the recipients were already cold."

The yellow beam swivels away, splashing against a stack of rusted oil drums.

"Go," Marlo says.

They move in unison, wheeling their heavy steel frames across the wet concrete. Leo’s boot slips on a patch of engine grease, but he recovers, sliding into the shadow of Cage Seven's heavy wire mesh.

Behind the iron grid, floor-to-ceiling shelving holds rows of unlabeled, wax-sealed parcels. Inside Leo's canvas courier bag, the rusted iron key vibrates with a sharp, rhythmic pulse, radiating an icy cold against his hip.

Marlo pulls out his keyring. The brass oval of Kallen's ID badge, *Courier 4409*, clinks against a heavy iron skeleton key.

"This unlocks the latch," Marlo whispers, pointing a scarred knuckle at a gray junction box wired directly into the brickwork. "But Gideon has an analog trip-wire plumbed into this cylinder. The second the bolt slides, his console upstairs registers the breach."

"He already knows we're off-route," Leo says. "The terminal logged the drop at 4B. We need those manifests."

Marlo doesn't argue. He slides the iron skeleton key into the keyway, the metal biting into the tumblers.

He puts his weight into the turn.

Instead of the lock disengaging, the cylinder snaps with a brittle, metallic crack, freezing solid in the keyway. Internal relays inside the gray box chatter violently, and the heavy mesh door of Cage Seven violently unlatches from the inside, swinging wide open into the dark corridor.
