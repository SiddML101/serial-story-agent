# Episode 8

> **Planned beat:** Leo confronts Gideon over the terminal's comm channel about delivering to dead people, but Gideon dismisses the complaint as a routine system glitch.

**Words:** 483 · **Version:** v2 · **Human edited:** no · **Revisions:** 0 · **Critic:** hook 4/5, momentum 4/5

**Hook (reversal):** The handlebar terminal abruptly redirects delivery 2/5 to Apartment 4B as Gideon's central dispatch console suffers a total external lock.  
**In-story time:** Day 2, 10:15 pm

---

"He didn't make it out of the building," Leo says, his voice a flat rasp.

Mira shines her small penlight onto the kitchen sink, illuminating the calcified lime crusting the brass faucet like dry white moss. "They moved him while he was still drawing breath, Leo. Look at the timeline. It’s a systemic scrub."

Leo presses the rubber-molded transmit button on his collar. "Gideon. We need to talk about the routing manifest."

The earpiece crackles, the signal carrying the faint, rhythmic *clack-clack* of high-speed sorting machines back at the FlashDrop hub. 

"You are stationary, Rider Vance," Gideon's voice is smooth, entirely free of the storm's urgency. "You have two minutes before the terminal initiates its secondary voltage check."

"The first parcel went to Harlan Reed," Leo says, keeping his back to the hallway door. "He was dead an hour before I got there. Now I’m in Kallen’s flat. His gear is still here, but his name is on a forged coroner's report. Explain the manifest."

A brief, heavy pause stretches over the static. 

"An administrative anomaly," Gideon says, his tone dismissive, almost bored. "The Shift Zero routing protocol pulls from an archived analog directory under the city mortuary. Old databases occasionally experience indexing cross-talk. It is a standard network caching error, nothing more."

"A caching error doesn't forge death certificates," Leo snaps. 

Mira steps closer, her fingers gripping her camera strap. "Ask him about 4B," she whispers. "Tell him we have the transfer slip."

"Your job is to move the cargo, Vance," Gideon says. "Not to audit the logistics. Clear Locker 412. The system will auto-correct once the queue is empty."

"And Kallen?" Leo demands. "He was routed to 4B while he was still breathing."

"The company has no record of that address," Gideon says, his voice sharpening. "You are chasing ghosts in a glitching network. Resume your route."

The connection doesn't just drop; it shatters.

A high-pitched, metallic squeal rips through Leo’s earpiece, forcing him to yank the plastic bud from his ear. On his handlebars, the terminal’s display screen begins to cascade with raw, green code. 

The letters *LOCKER 412* dissolve into a scrambled line of static. 

A single, bold line of text replaces it: *DELIVERY 2/5 REDIRECTED. APARTMENT 4B.*

The speaker on the terminal stem clicks. Gideon's voice comes through the tiny grill, but the cold, corporate polish is gone, replaced by a sudden, thin panic.

"Vance? What did you just input? My console just went black. The central dispatch terminal has initiated an external lock."

"I didn't touch it," Leo says.

"Get out of that stairwell," Gideon commands, his voice warping under a wave of low-frequency electronic hum. "The routing loop is overriding the hub. It’s pulling you in. Vance, do not—"

The terminal emits a sharp, wet pop, and the line dies completely, leaving only the silent, pulsing green glow of the screen directing Leo up the stairs.
